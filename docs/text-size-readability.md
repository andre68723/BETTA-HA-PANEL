# Text size and readability

How text roles, the text size steps and the 30 cm readability design basis work. Users find the short version in [Appearance](appearance.md#text-size); the rendering rules of the four styles are in the [appearance reference](appearance-implementation.md).

## Optional widget names

`show_name: false` hides a widget's own name in every appearance style and its editor
preview. The remaining value and controls use the released space; style-specific name
formatting still goes through `ui_style_title()` when visible. See
[widget anatomy](widget-anatomy.md#shared-name-visibility) for compatibility and layout rules.

## Text size reference

Every text asks for a role, never for a pixel size (`ui/theme/ui_typography.h`); the size is a pure function of the role, the board and the user's step
(`main/ui/text_scale.c`, tested in `tests/host/test_text_scale.c`). The scale has ten steps: 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 42, 48, 56, 64 px (+4 px per step up to 36,
then +6, +6, +8, +8; deliberately uneven, because a 6 px jump at 32 px is too coarse for a user increment). Fitting may use any even size between the board's
minimum and the preferred size, measured with a probe font; only the chosen size becomes a render font.

`effective step = clamp(clamp(role + board offset, floor, 9) + user step, floor, 9)`; the user step (0 Standard, 1 Larger, 2 Largest) comes after the floor, so
+1 never disappears into it.

| Board | Offset | Minimum | Density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Panel4 | 0 | 20 px | about 255 ppi, the reference at the 30 cm design distance |
| Tab5 | +1 | 20 px | about 294 ppi |
| 10-inch boards (panel10, paneljc) | 0 | 16 px (the fitting floor) | about 149 ppi |

| Role | Typical use | Panel4 and 10-inch | +1 | +2 | Tab5 | Tab5 +1 | Tab5 +2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| meta | age, date, hints | 20 | 24 | 28 | 24 | 28 | 32 |
| label | names, days, buttons, units | 24 | 28 | 32 | 28 | 32 | 36 |
| value | ordinary readings, states | 28 | 32 | 36 | 32 | 36 | 42 |
| key | prominent readings | 36 | 42 | 48 | 42 | 48 | 56 |
| hero | very large readings | 42 | 48 | 56 | 48 | 56 | 64 |

Tab5's +1 board offset at user step 0 gives each role within about 4 % of the physical size of the Panel4 (font px / ppi: meta 2.08 against 2.00 mm, value 2.77 against 2.79 mm). The 10-inch boards use the
Panel4's sizes, which is physically about 70 % larger (meta 3.4 against 2.0 mm) and suits the longer viewing distance of a wall panel; the real reading distance has not been
measured. A profile one step below (meta 16 px) was tried first and rejected: it made every former 20 px title and note 16 px on the paneljc, which the comparison with the former
bitmap fonts showed. Font px is not ink height: the simulator's `--dump-layout` reports the measured capital and digit heights.

**Tile caps.** A tile that is too small for the growth a step asks for keeps a smaller text: `text_scale_tile_cap(board, widget type, w, h)` is a table of the
smallest tile size per growth step (the energy page has its own row: growth only on a screen at least 1000 px wide). The table is
generated by `tools/calibrate_text_caps.py`, which renders every layout fixture (gallery, `layout_cases.json` with the sizes of real layouts, readability, room and energy
fixtures) at the largest step with the cap forced to 0 ... 3 and takes, per widget type, the thresholds below which a tile overlapped or clipped essential text. Run it
again after a layout change; widgets that lay out from line heights and keep one-line labels in dot mode grow in more sizes. `tools/check_widget_layout.py
--text-step 1|2` must stay clean. The runner checks panel4, Tab5 and paneljc geometry at step 0;
`--full` adds steps 1 and 2. It does not separately render all five firmware variants.

**Metrics.** `lv_tiny_ttf` takes line height and base line from the font; the font manager applies the metrics of the former bitmap fonts (about 1.25 em lines) so tiles lay out
as before, with the base line one pixel lower than the table value. Symbol, icon and emoji fonts have their own rows.

## Panel4 readability at 30 cm

Panel4 uses the role sizes of the table above (the reference density): 20 px metadata, 24 px names/days, 28 px ordinary readings and 36 px prominent sensor and
gauge readings. Values fit down through the even sizes to the 20 px minimum when a tile needs it; linear gauges can split a unit onto the next line. Saved rectangles are
retained. On the 4-inch class (panel4, Tab5) nothing is below 20 px and `tools/check_widget_layout.py` fails on any label below 20 px on those boards
(it also reports the overlaps the larger text causes).

Weather retains the existing number of days unless `forecast_days` is chosen.
Use 0 for measured auto or 1..7 explicitly (columns including today: up to six future days plus today). Weather and solar share this grid and its width, so "Line up days with the weather tile" holds for every count. Fewer wider columns are an optional
trade-off; they make room for low temperatures and configured rain. Long weather
reports remain in detail. Narrow columns may use the short weekday instead of
“Today”. Solar's `align_forecast: true` (vertical bars only) puts Rest in the header and aligns up to
seven dated columns with weather. Give both tiles equal x/width and day count.
The Solar detail popup retains every configured daily reading.

Design basis: the active area of the 4B display is 71.93 x 71.93 mm (Waveshare specification; not the 86.5 mm module
outline), 0.0999 mm per pixel or 254.25 ppi. The owner chose **30 cm** as the design distance (2026-09-28) and 40 cm as
an additional comparison. A capital height of 16 arcmin is the ergonomic minimum for essential text and 20-22 arcmin the
preferred range (ETSI EG 202 116 Table 17, summarizing ISO 9241-3; font size alone never makes a dashboard compliant):
`angle = 2 * atan(height_mm / (2 * distance_mm))` with `height_mm = ink_px * 71.93 / 720`.

| Role | Examples | Panel4 size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| meta | age, date, incidental hints | 20 px |
| label | names, days, required units, navigation | 24 px (20 px only as a reviewed compact fallback) |
| value | low, rain, daily kWh, range, important state | 28 px |
| key | main sensor reading | 36 px |
| hero | headline number | 42 px |

Units stay readable (24 px beside a 28/36 px number) and are never shrunk below 16 px (20 px on the 4-inch class) or dropped. The roles are
`main/ui/theme/ui_typography.h`.

The 10-inch paneljc stands on a desk or a chest of drawers at the same 30 cm. Its active area is 216.58 x 135.36 mm
([board specifications](hardware/paneljc.md#manufacturer-references)), so a pixel is 0.1692 mm (150 ppi, about 1.7 times the 4-inch pixel). Capital-height angles at 30 cm by
the same formula: Poppins 16 px (11 px ink) 21.3', 20 px 27.1', 24 px 33.0', 28 px 38.8', 38 px 50.4'; at 40 cm the 16 px text
is still 16.0'. The smallest text on the 10-inch is therefore already inside the preferred 20-22' range, which is why it needs no
role remapping like the 4-inch (geometric estimate, not a comfort test on the device). Its layouts are checked for fit with
`tools/check_widget_layout.py --variant paneljc`.

Actual Poppins Medium glyph metrics (antialiasing boxes, geometric estimates):

| Nominal px | Line px | H / x / 0 ink px | H arcmin at 30 cm | H arcmin at 40 cm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 25 | 14 / 11 / 15 | 16.03 | 12.02 |
| 24 | 32 | 17 / 14 / 19 | 19.46 | 14.60 |
| 28 | 35 | 20 / 16 / 21 | 22.90 | 17.17 |
| 38 (earlier key/hero size, not on the current scale; not re-measured for 36 and 42) | 47 | 26 / 21 / 28 | 29.77 | 22.32 |

The short Old School weather cards prioritize day and high at 24/28 px; their daily condition icons live in
the tappable day popup, while Küche's taller cards keep them. That trades icons, not the six-day horizon,
for larger readings.

See the [4-inch simulator example](pages.md#free-pages) for a dashboard preview. Pixel/glyph measurements support layout review;
comfort at 30 cm still needs the owner's physical display assessment.
